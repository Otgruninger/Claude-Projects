What UpAhead does that Brightspace Tracker doesn’t — and which parts are worth taking

UpAhead and Brightspace Tracker solve the same problem from opposite ends: one is a server-side account that students feed a syllabus to, the other is a browser extension that reads the LMS directly. That single architectural difference explains almost every feature gap between them.

01

What UpAhead actually is

A web app plus an iOS app, launched 10 January 2025 by Thomas Lentine, a University of South Carolina alumnus. Press from early 2025 puts it at roughly 500 registered users across 35 universities — so it is a comparable-stage indie product, not an incumbent. That matters: it is not out-executing you on resources, it has just made different bets.

The core promise is syllabus to schedule in 30 seconds. A student uploads, screenshots, or pastes a syllabus for each class; the app extracts graded assignments, due dates, the grading breakdown, class meeting times, and professor office hours, then merges every syllabus into one semester schedule the student can review and tweak. From there it runs three engines on top of that data: a grade calculator, an outbound reminder system, and calendar sync.

The strategic thing to notice is that the syllabus is the onboarding, not the product. LMS connection is secondary for them — they import your school’s ICS calendar feed, which is shallow data. Your extension reads the gradebook and course content directly. You have better raw material and are doing less with it.

The build order

Ranked, and the ranking is the argument: each item is chosen partly because it unlocks the ones below it. Effort assumes you are working solo on an existing MV3 extension.

01

A synced account behind the extension

Effort large Impact unlocks 03, 04, 06, 07 Type architecture
The move

Demote the extension from database to client. It keeps doing what only it can do — scrape the authenticated Brightspace session — and pushes a normalized copy of courses, items, due dates, and grades to a server. The server becomes what everything else hangs off.

This is the whole ballgame. Every remaining gap in the matrix above is downstream of not having a server-side copy of the data: no SMS, no email digest, no live calendar feed, no phone access, no parent view, no streaks that survive a reinstall.

Watch for
  • It costs you your best marketing line. “No data collection” stops being literally true. Do not fudge it — reframe as opt-in: local-only stays the default, sync is a switch the student flips to get reminders, and say plainly what leaves the browser.
  • Sync one direction first (extension → server). Bidirectional edit merging is a later problem, not a launch problem.
  • Store the minimum: item titles, types, due dates, points, weights. No page content, no PDFs, no session cookies server-side, ever.
  • MV3 service workers get killed aggressively — drive sync from chrome.alarms plus a scrape on Brightspace page visits, not a long-lived timer.
02

Weighted grade engine with target-score projection

Effort medium Impact highest single feature Type feature
The move

“What do I need on the final to keep my A?” is the question students actually open a tracker to answer, and UpAhead built its whole grade experience around it: a live weighted grade, a per-category contribution breakdown, and a solved-for target score.

You can do this strictly better than they can. Their grades are whatever the student typed in or whatever a syllabus implied. Yours come out of the real Brightspace gradebook — actual scores, actual category weights, actual dropped-lowest rules. Same feature, no data entry, and correct.

Watch for
  • Pull grade items and category weights from the gradebook while the student is signed in; fall back to weights parsed from the syllabus when a professor hasn’t configured them.
  • Handle the ugly cases honestly — ungraded items, extra credit, drop-lowest, unweighted point totals. Show the assumption you made rather than a confidently wrong number.
  • Copy the circular dial with percentage and letter grade. It is a small piece of UI doing a lot of work, and letter grade is what students actually think in.
  • Ship the projection as a slider, not a form. Dragging a target and watching required scores move is the demo that sells the product.
03

A published ICS subscription URL per student

Effort small Impact high Type feature
The move

UpAhead advertises push into Google, Apple, and Outlook. You do not need three OAuth integrations to match that — you need one authenticated ICS endpoint per user. All three clients subscribe to ICS, and a subscription stays live, so every scrape silently updates the student’s real calendar.

Best effort-to-payoff ratio on this list by a wide margin. It is a serialization function and a signed URL, and it neutralizes an entire row of their feature table.

Watch for
  • Make the URL an unguessable token, revocable and rotatable from settings — calendar subscriptions cannot send auth headers, so the URL is the credential.
  • Stable UID per LMS item so edits update the event instead of duplicating it.
  • Timed items as timed events, all-day deadlines as all-day; set VALARM defaults; carry the course code in the title so it reads well in a crowded week view.
  • Google refreshes subscribed calendars slowly and on its own schedule. Say so in the UI before someone files a bug.
04

Outbound reminders — email first, SMS later

Effort small (email) / medium (SMS) Impact high Type feature
The move

Their reminders reach a student who has not opened anything; a Chrome notification only reaches one who already has Chrome running. Match the pattern with a daily digest plus per-item lead-time reminders, both server-side.

Do email first. It is a cron job and a template, needs no phone number, and covers most of the value. SMS is the upgrade, and it is the part with real friction.

Watch for
  • US A2P SMS requires 10DLC brand and campaign registration — real paperwork, real per-message cost, and carrier filtering if you skip it. Budget weeks, not an afternoon. This is likely why their SMS is a paid-tier feature, and it should be yours too.
  • Let students choose lead times per item type. An exam wants a week; a discussion post wants an evening.
  • One digest at a chosen hour beats per-item pings. Over-notification is the fastest route to an uninstall.
  • Include a genuine one-click unsubscribe in every send.
05

Upgrade syllabus parsing from dates to structure

Effort medium Impact feeds 02 Type feature
The move

You already scrape syllabus PDFs for deadlines. UpAhead pulls three more things out of the same document: the grading breakdown with category weights, class meeting times, and professor office hours. The weights are the important one — they are what makes recommendation 02 work when a professor never configured the Brightspace gradebook, which is often.

Meeting times and office hours are cheap once you are already parsing, and they turn the product from a deadline list into the thing a student opens to answer “where am I supposed to be, and when can I get help.”

Watch for
  • Also accept screenshots and pasted text, not just PDFs. Plenty of syllabi live inside a Brightspace content page, and your extension can grab that page for free.
  • Always show a review-and-edit step. Their copy leans on this too, and it converts a parsing miss from a broken product into a five-second correction.
  • Reconcile against what you scraped from the LMS rather than importing blind — dedupe on title similarity plus date proximity, and prefer the LMS date when they disagree.
06

Parent view with granular, revocable consent

Effort medium Impact new revenue channel Type feature + business model
The move

The most commercially interesting thing on their site, and the least obvious. The student invites a parent by email, then controls three independent switches — due dates and progress, course schedule, grades — with grades behind an extra confirmation and everything revocable from the profile. They wrap it in FERPA-aligned, consent-driven language and sell a “Gift + Dashboard” plan where the parent pays.

That last part solves the hardest problem in student software: students are broke and parents are not. A parent buying a semester for their kid is a far easier sale than a student buying it for themselves.

Watch for
  • Consent must be genuinely student-held and revocable, and the parent must never see more than the switches allow. Copy their structure closely — it is well designed and the failure mode here is reputational.
  • FERPA binds institutions, not you, but the framing is doing real work with parents. Do not overclaim compliance; describe the mechanism instead.
  • Requires recommendation 01 and a small web surface. This is the point at which you are no longer only a Chrome extension.
  • Add the audience landing page to match — a /parents page is how this gets found at all.
07

The daily loop: today view, streaks, completion ritual

Effort small Impact retention Type UX
The move

You ship four views; they ship one opinionated default. Their daily view leads with overdue work, then upcoming, with a streak counter — plus To Do / Completed tabs, swipe to complete, and confetti on finishing. Their release notes lead with the confetti, which tells you what they think retains people.

A tracker that only shows deadlines is consulted twice a semester. A tracker with a satisfying daily check-in gets opened every morning, and habit is what makes a semester license renew.

Watch for
  • Make Today the default view, not one of four equals. Overdue at the top, in the alarm color.
  • Completion needs to feel like something — a real animation, a visible count moving. This is the cheapest feature on the list and punches above its weight.
  • Streaks should count “checked in” days, not “completed everything” days. Punishing a heavy week is how you lose the habit you just built.
  • Keep the other three views for people who found them. This is about the default, not about removal.
08

Fix the pricing page contradiction, then borrow their page structure

Effort tiny Impact conversion Type marketing
The move

Your own store and site copy currently says both “renews each semester — cancel anytime” and “one payment, license never expires.” Those are different products. A student reading both does not know what they are buying, and ambiguity at the price is where checkouts die. Pick one and say it once.

Then take their site shape: a dedicated page per audience — /students, /parents, /pricing — each written to one reader with one ask, rather than a single homepage trying to address everyone.

Watch for
  • Your semester license is arguably the better model for a single-LMS tool — it matches how students actually think about time. Lean into it as a contrast with their monthly subscription rather than converting to match.
  • Lead with Gradescope import somewhere prominent. It is a genuine differentiator they have no answer to, and it is currently buried.
  • Skip the programmatic /testimonials/* pattern — see below.
05

What not to copy

Multi-LMS sprawl

Supporting Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle, and D2L is why their integration is a shallow ICS feed — breadth forced them to the lowest common denominator. Your entire advantage is Brightspace depth and “it auto-detects your school.” Going multi-LMS trades a real moat for a feature checkbox.

Programmatic testimonial SEO

Their /testimonials/* URLs carry slugs about micro-investing — content unrelated to the product, generated at scale. That pattern is thin-content risk with search engines and reads badly to any student who lands on one. Do not import it.

“AI” as the headline

Their App Store name leads with “AI Assignment Tracker.” Your promise is stronger and more concrete: it already knows your classes because it reads your actual Brightspace. Sell the outcome, not the technique — the technique is table stakes now.

Monthly subscription for a semester-shaped product

$9.99/mo bills students through summer, when they use nothing. Your semester license fits the actual usage pattern and is easier to justify at the point of sale. Keep it — just state it unambiguously.

06

One architectural warning

Everything above assumes you keep reading Brightspace through the student’s own authenticated browser session. Do not be tempted toward the D2L Valence API as a “proper” replacement: it requires per-institution app keys issued by each school’s Brightspace administrator, which is not obtainable at indie scale across hundreds of universities. The extension approach is not a hack you should outgrow — it is the only path to “works at any school, day one,” and it is precisely what UpAhead cannot replicate.

The correct shape, then, is a thin server that never holds a Brightspace credential and never talks to Brightspace itself. The extension is the only thing with access; the server only ever receives already-extracted, normalized items. That keeps the privacy story defensible after recommendation 01, and it keeps the trust boundary somewhere you can describe in one sentence to a student.
